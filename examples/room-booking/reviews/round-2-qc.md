- [MAJOR] requirements-specification.md §10 NFR-26 (statement and AC (b)), §13.2 AS-34; research-report.md F-34 and C-18 — NFR-26 sets no overall cap on consecutive failed sign-ins — The statement caps consecutive failures at 5, but the block lifts after 15 minutes. AC (b) only requires "no more than 5 for that card reached the LMS in any 15 minutes", so a product can pass while allowing 20 PIN guesses an hour (480 a day) on one card indefinitely. If the LMS PIN is 4 digits (its format isn't recorded), that covers all 10,000 combinations in about 21 days. AS-34 and C-18 justify the 5 as "far inside NIST's ceiling of 100", but NIST's 100 is a total at which sign-in is disabled, not a rate: NIST SP 800-63B-4 §3.2.2 [S76] says the verifier "SHALL limit consecutive failed authentication attempts … to no more than 100 by disabling that authenticator", which must then be re-bound to the account. F-34 omits "by disabling that authenticator". The NCSC's preferred throttling is "a progressively increasing time delay" [S85], not a fixed block that repeats. This gap came from QC's own round-1 uplift wording, which the BA implemented as written. It is raised now because the fetched text of §3.2.2 shows the design falls short of the standard it cites.

Add AC (d): "Given a test card, when 20 wrong PINs are entered across successive blocks, then a later attempt with the correct PIN is refused, however long after, until staff re-enable sign-in for that card; afterwards the correct PIN succeeds." Rewrite AC (b): "Given council IT's scripted run of 100 wrong attempts on one card, waiting out each block, then no more than 20 checks for that card reach the LMS in total."
