# Research report — <analysis title>

> Template. Every `## ` heading below is required (the orchestrator's pre-check looks for each). Keep the
> headings' wording; you may number them ("## 1. Executive summary"). Write "Not applicable — <reason>"
> under a heading that genuinely doesn't apply.

## Executive summary
One page: the question, what the evidence says, the recommendation it supports, and the confidence.

## Context and objectives
The problem as the brief and the owner's answers state it; what this research had to establish.

## Research questions
Numbered RQ-1, RQ-2, … Each is answered in Findings or listed in Limitations with the reason it stays open.

## Method
Where you searched, inclusion criteria (credibility, recency), how sources were verified (each fetched
before being cited), and the date range covered.

## Findings
One subsection per research question. Every factual claim cites the register as `[S#]`; every key
finding has at least two independent sources. Mark estimates as estimates.

## Comparables and alternatives
Existing products, services, processes or approaches, including "do nothing": what they do, strengths,
weaknesses, and what it means for this analysis.

## Regulatory, standards and accessibility context
Legislation, regulation, standards and accessibility obligations for the domain and jurisdictions in
scope, with correct titles and versions.

## Risks and counter-evidence
Evidence that cuts against the recommendation, and risks the research surfaced.

## Limitations
What the research could not establish, and why.

## Conclusions and implications for requirements
What the findings mean for the requirements specification, each conclusion citing its findings.

## Sources
The source register. One line per source, numbered in citation order:
`- [S1] <Title> — <Publisher/author>, <publication date>. <URL> (accessed YYYY-MM-DD)`
Owner-provided material: `- [S2] <Title> — provided by the owner (<file name>), <date>.`
Every entry is cited at least once; every citation resolves here.
